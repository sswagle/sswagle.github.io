5G/6G & Smart Networks

5G has evolved mobile connectivity from simply connecting people to connecting devices, industries, vehicles, cities and critical services. It enables applications such as IoT, connected mobility, smart manufacturing and low-latency services.

6G (IMT-2030) is the next evolution, aiming to combine communication, sensing, computing and AI. Key areas include ubiquitous connectivity, immersive communication, AI-enabled networks, integrated sensing and communication, sustainability, security and resilience.

Smart Networks go beyond connectivity: they dynamically use AI, edge/cloud computing, sensing and automation to optimise how data and services are delivered.

Terrestrial & Non-Terrestrial Networks

Terrestrial networks — such as cellular, fibre and Wi-Fi — provide the core connectivity for populated and industrial areas.

Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN) — including satellites and high-altitude platforms — extend connectivity to remote areas, oceans, disaster zones and locations without reliable terrestrial infrastructure.

The convergence of terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks is becoming increasingly important, including emerging direct-to-device satellite services.

Why It Matters to Society and the Market

The market is moving towards seamless, intelligent and ubiquitous connectivity. This is important for:

  • Smart mobility and connected vehicles
  • Healthcare and emergency response
  • Smart agriculture and logistics
  • Connected industry and manufacturing
  • Digital inclusion and remote connectivity

The combination of 5G/6G, terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks can provide connectivity anywhere, anytime, while supporting increasingly intelligent digital services.

Key Message

The future is not simply 6G replacing 5G; it is the convergence of terrestrial, satellite, AI, sensing and computing into intelligent networks serving people, industries and society.
